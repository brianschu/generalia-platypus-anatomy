SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE
Established physiology and behaviour:
Gregory JE, Iggo A, McIntyre AK, Proske U (1988), Receptors in the bill of the platypus, Journal of Physiology 400:349–366, DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.1988.sp017124. Electroreceptors respond to changing electrical signals; bill mechanoreceptor types have different adaptation properties. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1191811/
Fjällbrant TT, Manger PR, Pettigrew JD (1998), Some related aspects of platypus electroreception: temporal integration behaviour, electroreceptive thresholds and directionality of the bill acting as an antenna, Philosophical Transactions B 353:1211–1219, DOI 10.1098/rstb.1998.0277. Observed responses include temporal integration of repeated electrical stimuli; field-pattern/direction/range analyses are discussed with theoretical alternatives. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1692301/
Australian Museum, Platypus species account: eyes, ears and nostrils close when foraging underwater; shrimp are part of the diet; invertebrate prey is collected from the bottom, stored in cheek pouches and chewed at the surface using grinding plates. https://australian.museum/learn/animals/mammals/platypus/

Bethge P, Munks S, Nicol S (2001), Energetics of foraging and locomotion in the platypus Ornithorhynchus anatinus, Journal of Comparative Physiology B 171:497–506, DOI 10.1007/s003600100200. In a swim tank, minimum subsurface transport cost was found at 0.4 m/s. V6 now uses 0.4 m/s as steady forward underwater cruise, with acceleration and slower sifting/backward movement. This is a research-based choice, not a universal measured speed of wild hunting animals. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11585262/
Fish FE et al. (1997), Energetics of swimming by the platypus: metabolic effort associated with rowing, Journal of Experimental Biology 200:2647–2652. Flume trials covered 0.45–1.0 m/s. Those experimental speeds should not be substituted for typical wild foraging speed. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9359371/
Pettigrew JD, Manger PR, Fine SL (1998), The sensory world of the platypus, Philosophical Transactions B 353:1199–1210, DOI 10.1098/rstb.1998.0276. Describes variable head/bill movements and more active searching after prey-related contact. V6 supplies automatic underwater sweeps, with temporary manual override; its cadence/amplitude and contact modulation are designed animation values. We do not claim an experimentally measured fixed continuous scan rhythm. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1692312/

RESEARCH-INFORMED SIMULATION
The electrical source is an idealized quasistatic point-dipole proxy, assuming a homogeneous isotropic conducting medium. This assumed field equation is not a finding of the physiology sources above. Its field is with E proportional to [3 rhat(p dot rhat) - p]/r^3, regularized with r^2 + 0.08 game units squared near the source. Each displayed bill point samples a projection of that vector. A twitch envelope makes a brief pulse plus a faint resting source. Shrimp muscle waveforms, source magnitude, orientation and decay in real sediment are not calibrated here. This abstraction produces a varying spatial pattern rather than a simple distance meter. Display smoothing is a gameplay filter, not a quantitative neural integration model.
The mechanical field uses a separate delayed twitch envelope with a decaying amplitude. Lag, gain, travel time, background current and contact thresholds are chosen for play. In particular, mechanical/electrical timing is not presented as an experimentally verified biological rangefinder. Gravel proximity/contact contributes yellow signals without blue. The inset uses the actual bill mesh, but its regular dots are schematic samples, not a receptor-count or anatomical distribution map. Blue and yellow lights on both views are teaching overlays, not naturally luminous anatomy.
The model remains in metres internally; a 2.1 presentation scale places it in the inherited V4 game world. Scene dimensions and the 55-second breath allowance are gameplay values. Signal amplitudes have no microvolt or neural-rate units. Colours, caustics, underwater fill lighting and observer camera are artistic teaching choices.

REVISION DETAILS
Forage here replaces the opaque Probe label. It lowers the bill, slows movement, stirs sediment and adds contact activity; it collects shrimp only when the physical bill tip is in reach. It does not reveal prey or remotely scan. The cue panel explains when to use it.
The new dive camera holds a close view while the eyes close, then descends and returns to the third-person view. Eyelid patches and eye compression are V6-only approximate renderer additions; the native source has no facial rig. The source model remains unchanged. Closure is not a validated anatomical eyelid simulation.
Underwater uses stronger dark green fog, lower exposure, local animal fill and a gentle dark vignette. Visibility is intentionally limited. The inset uses separate bright educational lighting, rather than implying the animal has optical vision underwater.
Capture has an immediate announcement; after cheek storage, surfacing and chewing, the hunt earns a dedicated achievement screen. The procedural shrimp specimen is illustrative, not a reviewed species-specific asset. Replay randomizes its concealed patch.
Pointer lock is disabled and any stale lock is released. Scene dragging steers the head; the mouse is free to reach buttons. Tests clicked real bottom-right controls and checked touch hit targets. Automation could not inspect the user's actual in-app tab due to a sandbox setup error; a separate Edge browser was used successfully.

KNOWN LIMITS AND NEXT PRIORITIES
This is a working V6 checkpoint, not a claim that the exceptional visual/biological quality target is finished. The native asset's open foot/production-rig review is the main constraint on fully credible motion. Next: resolve the foot/web structure in a V6-only asset derivative, build/export a reviewed production rig and smaller mesh LOD, improve head/bill/capture and chewing detail, refine bank geology and vegetation placement, add depth-aware refraction, validate receptor/gameplay tuning against primary research and user playtesting, and profile actual phones plus additional browsers. Whole-animal contact is currently approximated by the body's centre near pool cobbles; probing adds silt and tactile activity without physically simulating every gravel grain. Sand ripples/caustics are artistic. No multiplayer, scoring economy, additional prey species or audio system is included.
