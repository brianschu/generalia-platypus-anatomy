CHANGES IN THIS PROTOTYPE

Geometry kept; skeletal groups colored.

  • Imported the source FBX in Blender and exported its evaluated mesh as a static GLB.
  • Applied a neutral ivory material for inspection. Original material assignments remain in the saved Blender inspection scene.
  • Excluded the original rig, skinning, and animation from the static GLB.
  • Added independently reviewed surface annotations for eleven gross skeletal groups. Ivory and anatomy colors use the same skeleton geometry.
  • Kept uncertain contacts gray. No fine sutures, individual constituent boundaries or serial bone identities were invented.

The original animation rig does not identify anatomical bones. The color review approves visible gross surface groups; it does not certify individual-bone segmentation, specimen identity or the living-to-skeleton fit.

LIVING EXTERIOR / SEPARATE INSPECTION

An illustrative reconstruction under review.

Living source inspection displays the existing Generalia exterior supplied with this project. It represents an adult male composite, not a scanned living counterpart of the Toulouse skeleton. Foot morphology and final appearance remain under review.

The copied GLB geometry and base-color textures are unchanged. This inspection uses studio lighting and reduced coat normal, specular and sheen response. The shared coat/foot-skin material also receives this response change; it does not correct foot structure. Native underfur differs from the browser guard-hair representation.

ANATOMICAL COLOR CONVENTIONS

Referenced conventions, and separate proposals.

Jaimi Gray publishes distinct colors for identified skeletal elements. The reviewed humerus surface family uses the documented humerus blue. Forearms remain a combined group, so the individual radius and ulna colors are not assigned.

The skull examples are reference conventions. Current skull and jaw surfaces use one Generalia composite hue because fine sutures are unresolved.

PROPOSED GENERALIA EXTENSIONS / SEPARATE FROM THE SOURCE TABLES

The other group hues are Generalia display extensions. The hand complex borrows Gray's carpal hue as a composite proposal; it does not claim that the entire hand is carpal bone. The foot complex similarly uses the project's proposed tarsal hue. Shoulder and pelvic colors identify whole complexes with unresolved constituents.

Biodiversity's annotated platypus was used as a scientific and presentation benchmark only. Its geometry was not reused. This preview offers reviewed gross group colors; its source detail and constituent segmentation do not match that benchmark.
